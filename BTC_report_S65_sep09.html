⚠ A known bug class recurred — caught by the alignment assertion, before publication
The first run of today's persistence computation reported the three resolutions beating the baseline by +0.58, +0.63 and +0.55. Those numbers were wrong. The correct results are −0.8759, −0.6701 and −1.0213 — all three LOSE.
The cause is the August 14 band-index bug, recurring. The band-membership helper returns an ascending price index (0 = lowest band); the probability vectors are written descending (index 0 = Band A, the highest). The two conventions are exact mirrors — ascending + descending = 3 — so writing one into the other places the persistence mass on the wrong band. Today it put 57.1% on Band B when the reference band was C, inflating the naive Brier from 0.2449 to 1.1020 and flipping three losses into three apparent wins.
The Aug 14 correction said: "every persistence computation now asserts index alignment before scoring." That assertion was not executed today. The rule existed, was written down, and was skipped — which is a worse failure than not having the rule. It was caught only because the reinstated baseline produced results implausibly favourable to an instrument with a 1-in-10 record, and the figure was re-derived by hand.
This is the third bug of this family: ascending-vs-descending band indices (Aug 14), open-bounded residual matching every outcome (S56), and now the first recurring. Adopted: band membership is computed by a single function that returns the probability-vector slot directly, with the ascending helper deleted rather than kept alongside it. Two conventions coexisting in one codebase is the defect; asserting between them was never going to hold.
