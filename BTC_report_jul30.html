✱ Is the plan changing?
No — the position doesn't change, but the reason for caution does. The single binary this framework had been deferring to for two weeks has now resolved: the Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on July 29 by a 9–3 vote, with all three dissents arguing for a hike. Bitcoin's response is the most informative datum of the session — it did not break. The Dow fell about 1,100 points in its worst day in over a year and the 30-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2007, yet BTC closed the day up at $63,983 and has since traded to $64,523. That inverts the negative cross-asset divergence this framework logged on Jul 27–28.
Structurally, the floor did its job for a fourth time. Jul 28 printed a low of $62,726.80 — the deepest probe since Jul 17 — and held above the $62,589 confluence top by 0.22%, on 1.63× average volume, followed by a higher low and a higher close on Jul 29 at 1.53× volume. Sixteen consecutive settled closes now sit above $62,457, the lowest of them $63,750.88. The ceiling, meanwhile, is untouched: $65,000 has produced six closes above it in twenty-two sessions and only one multi-day run (Jul 20–23), which then failed its backtest.
So the picture is a genuine coil, not a resolution: a floor that has been defended repeatedly on expanding volume, a ceiling that has rejected three separate attempts, and a 20-day SMA at $64,492 that has now converged into the same $64,500–$65,400 band as the 50- and 100-day averages, stacking resistance directly between price and the gate. Confidence moves 58 → 59/100 and aggressiveness 2 → 3/10. That is a deliberately small move: the honest reading is that the FOMC relief was largely pre-priced, and the temptation to reward a resolved binary with a bigger confidence jump is exactly the narrative pull the scorecard below exists to resist.
