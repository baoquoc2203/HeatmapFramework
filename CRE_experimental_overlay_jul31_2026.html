Step 2 · The six questions answered
1 · Do they lean bullish, bearish or neutral?
Mercury: neutral-directional but anti-breakout. Its standard specification says nothing about direction — it says moves fail. Applied to a range-bound market, "moves fail" mechanically means "the range holds," which penalises the floor-break and the upside break equally.
Lunar: mildly bearish into approximately Aug 12. Waning gibbous is a declining-momentum phase under the standard reading, with the Jul 29 Full Moon as a local top marker and the Aug 12 New Moon as the next candidate reversal point.
2 · How would they change the interpretation?
Only marginally, and mostly by offsetting each other. Mercury pushes toward "nothing resolves"; Lunar pushes toward "it resolves downward." Those pull in opposite directions on three of five branches. The compressed net effect — a maximum 2-point move — is the single most informative output of this exercise. Two layers applied at full permitted strength cannot move a genuinely ambiguous structural read.
3 · Which scenario would they reinforce?
They split. Mercury reinforces range continuation (+4). Lunar reinforces floor break (+3). Each ends up +1 after the other's opposition. Neither is meaningfully strengthened.
4 · Which scenario would they weaken?
Upside resolution — and this is their only point of agreement. Mercury penalises it as a breakout (−1); Lunar penalises it as counter-momentum during a waning phase (−1). Net −2, from 8% to 6%. If the overlay carries any information at all, it is concentrated in this one branch: both layers say a clean break above $65,150 in the next seven sessions is the least likely path. Notably, the official model already ranked it last.
5 · Would they shift the preferred timing window?
The core Oct–Nov 2026 window is unchanged — it is anchored to halving-interval arithmetic, which the overlay does not touch. But the overlay does generate near-term texture the official model does not have:

• Aug 6–7 — post-retrograde shadow closes; under the specification, the first "clean" decision window
• Aug 12 — New Moon; candidate local reversal marker
• Oct 24 – Nov 13 — the third Mercury retrograde of 2026 falls squarely inside the core cycle-bottom window

That last item is the overlay's most interesting product. Under its own logic, a bottom printing inside a retrograde would be expected to arrive as a violent false breakdown — which maps onto a Wyckoff spring — with any confirmation signal treated as unreliable until after Nov 13. That is a specific, checkable claim, and it is more than the official model says about the internal shape of that window.
6 · More aggressive or more defensive?
Marginally more defensive — but it hits the floor. Both layers argue for caution; the official posture is already WAIT at 2/10. The overlay's practical output is therefore not "reduce size" but "prefer to act after Aug 6–12 rather than before," which is a timing preference layered onto an already-defensive stance.
Step 3 · Explaining the differences
The differences are small, and that is the finding. Given full permitted strength — a ±5pp cap on every branch, applied to both layers simultaneously — the overlay moved the tree by 1, 1, 0, −2 and 0 points, confidence by one point, and aggressiveness not at all. Nothing crossed a decision threshold. Every gate in the official report resolves identically under the overlay: BUY 3 held, BUY 1 held, AERO gate unarmed, WAIT enforced, invalidation levels untouched.

Why so small? Because the two layers' standard specifications are structurally opposed in a range-bound market. Mercury's claim is about reliability (moves fail → ranges persist). Lunar's claim is about direction (waning → down). A layer that says "nothing resolves" and a layer that says "it resolves downward" cancel almost exactly when the market is sitting between a ceiling and a floor. The compression is not an artifact of a cautious cap — it is a consequence of applying both specifications honestly at the same time.

The one signal worth remembering: the only concurrence is the downgrade of the upside branch, and the official model had already ranked that branch last on structural grounds alone. The overlay's single agreement point is a conclusion the baseline reached without it.
Specification sensitivity — the honest caveat. These numbers are a function of the rules fixed in Step 0, not of the sky. Defensible alternative specifications exist and some flip signs: treating post-shadow as already cleared rather than active removes Mercury's contribution entirely; reading waning gibbous as "pressure released after the Full Moon" rather than "declining momentum" flips Lunar from −3/+3 to roughly +3/−3, which would produce an experimental tree of 44 / 27 / 14 / 6 / 5 — leaning the opposite way. The overlay's output is therefore a function of an unconstrained modelling choice. That is precisely the property a pre-registered backtest would remove, and precisely why the official model does not carry these layers today.
What would make this section promotable
Three things, none of which require abandoning the exercise: (a) fix one specification in advance and never revise it; (b) run it against multi-year BTC history and measure breakout-failure rates inside vs. outside retrograde and by lunar octile, with confidence intervals; (c) pre-register the threshold that would count as a pass — for example, a ≥10pp deviation from base rate that survives out-of-sample. If those clear, the layer earns weight on evidence and the official model takes it. If they don't, this section stays exactly where it is: interesting, quarantined, and clearly labelled.
