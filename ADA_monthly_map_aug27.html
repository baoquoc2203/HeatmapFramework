Register, rule change & scoring — new structural-read standard adopted this session

Rule adopted — the two-close standard now governs structural reads, not just stops.
Statement: a structural interpretation may not change rank on a single settled close. Rank changes require either (a) two consecutive settled closes on the same side of the governing level, or (b) a pre-registered discriminator firing.
Why: the two-consecutive-close standard has governed the thesis stop, entries and invalidations since the campaign began, and it is why the stop has never fired on a probe. It has never governed interpretation. Over Aug 25–26 a single close moved the lead read twice, and both moves were reversed by the next candle.
Applied retroactively: the Aug 26 flip to distribution-lead should not have happened. The correct handling was to hold both reads near-even and wait. That is this report's position at 50/50.
What it does not do: it does not slow down the discriminators. A settled close below $0.20694 or at/above $0.23332 still resolves the question in one candle, because those levels were pre-registered for exactly that purpose. The rule constrains narrative rank, not mechanical triggers.

Why the discriminator holding twice matters more than the floor breaking once. The $0.20694 level was swept 0.27% on Aug 25 and 2.15% on Aug 26 — eight times deeper — and produced a higher close on the second attempt ($0.21240 vs $0.20911). A level that absorbs a progressively deeper probe and returns a better close is behaving as support. Combined with five consecutive contracting volume sessions (34.11M → 8.39M, −75.4%), the evidence available today leans against distribution — which is precisely why the rank is suspended rather than flipped the other way. Leaning is not confirming, and this session's whole lesson is about not confusing the two.

Volatility methodology changed, and disclosed. The forward monthly cone now uses full-window σ = 3.7496%/day (n=44, Jul 14 – Aug 26; annualising to 71.6%) in place of σ20d = 4.3210%. Reason: √t extrapolation of a 20-day window inflated by the Aug 21–22 climax produced a Dec 2028 p95 of $1.69 against a p05 of $0.026 — a 64× span. That is arithmetic, not a forecast, and it would have rendered the whole 2027–28 half of the map meaningless. Near-term 7-day work still uses σ20d, which is the appropriate window for a 7-day horizon. Changing a method mid-series is a real risk; it is flagged here and in §08 rather than made silently.

How buy/sell months are assigned — and what they are not. Labels come from dated cycle anchors only. Oct–Nov 2026 from the 363–376 day post-peak interval off BTC's Oct 6, 2025 top. Rules the engine commits to. (1) Bands pre-registered and MECE. (2) Resolution mechanical and single-feed, no judgement at resolution. (3) A forecast is never revised after issue — only superseded by a new ID, with the original still resolving and scoring. (4) Probabilities sum to 100%; confidence reported separately. (5) Nothing outside the horizon influences the probabilities — the October window is excluded from all three live forecasts. (6) Misses are logged in the register before being explained anywhere.

Resolution mechanics. Settled 00:00 UTC daily close on Crypto.com ADA_USDT 1D. Fallback: CoinGecko daily close matched by price value, not date label (its labels run one day ahead of true UTC periods). A close within ±0.15% of a band boundary resolves VOID and is excluded rather than adjudicated.

Scoring — Brier score, skill score, calibration

Multi-category Brier. BS = Σᵢ (pᵢ − oᵢ)², oᵢ = 1 for the realised band, 0 otherwise. Range 0–2, lower is better. Worked for F-ADA-0003 at 37/27/36 — if BULL resolves, BS = 0.5834; if NEUTRAL, 0.8054; if BEAR, 0.5954.

Skill score. 1 − BS/BSref against a uniform ⅓-each reference, BSref = 0.667. Below 0.667 on a single observation beats the uniform baseline.

Calibration. Forecasts bucketed by stated probability against realised frequency — if the 30–40% bucket resolves true about 35% of the time, the engine is calibrated. Reliability and resolution decomposed separately, because a forecaster can be perfectly calibrated and still useless if every forecast is 33/33/33. Discrimination is the harder test.
