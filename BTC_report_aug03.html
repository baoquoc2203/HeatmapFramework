✱ Is the plan changing?
Yes — the read changes, the position does not. On July 31 Bitcoin poked $65,411 intraday, 0.63% above the ceiling, then reversed through the entire range in one session to close at $62,898 — a 4.70% traverse, 1.89× ATR, closing at just 15% of the day's range on 1.50× average volume. That is a textbook Wyckoff upthrust, and it is the single most consequential structural event since the July 20 SOS.
What makes this session different from an ordinary bad week is that the framework pre-registered the conditions under which it would change its mind — and all three fired at once. The July 30 report tilted the primary read to re-accumulation on exactly three asymmetries, and named them: the range low had not been retested since July 13; the lows were rising; and the expanding-volume sessions were closing up. As of August 1, the low has been retested and swept, the lows are falling again ($62,727 → $62,260), and the one expanding-volume session since — July 31, at 1.50× — closed sharply down. Three for three.
So the split moves, and it moves a long way: the primary and alternate Wyckoff reads are now tied at 45/45. Under the framework's own Uncertainty Mandate a tie is not a bearish call — it is an explicit instruction to stop taking directional risk and let the pre-committed triggers do the work. Confidence drops 59 → 48/100, aggressiveness 3 → 1/10, structural risk to High.
What has not happened matters just as much. There is still no settled close below $62,457 — the invalidation counter reads 0 of 2. July 31 closed at $62,898 and August 1 at $62,824, both above the entire confluence band, and both cross-verified or clearing the margin rule. July itself closed green, +7.2%, the first positive month since April. And the two sessions following the upthrust came on collapsing volume (0.46× and 0.44×) — an upthrust not yet followed by expanding-volume supply is an incomplete distribution, though that reading is confounded by the weekend and should not be leaned on.
The action is therefore HOLD BUY 3 — no add, and equally no reduce. Cutting here would be front-running a stop that has not triggered, which is the precise discipline failure the two-consecutive-close rule exists to prevent. The rule was validated in real time on July 13, when a single close below the floor reversed +4.33% the next day. It gets honoured now too, when honouring it is uncomfortable.

Quant PM & CRO note — two disciplines under pressure
On not tightening the gate. The Jul 31 upthrust is good evidence that a single close above $65,000 is a lower-quality signal than it was when the rule was written. The correct response is not to raise the bar today. Changing a pre-committed entry rule immediately after an adverse event is how a framework quietly converts discipline into hindsight. The proposal is therefore logged for pre-registration against future signals only: from the next session, consider requiring a settled close above the $65,411 upthrust high rather than $65,000, since a reclaim above the failure point is what actually negates an upthrust. That change takes effect only if adopted before a signal fires, never after.
On not front-running the stop. With price 0.59% above the invalidation level and the two reads tied, the emotional pull is to reduce now and ask questions later. The counter-argument is the framework's own record: the two-consecutive-close rule has been tested four times (Jul 13, Jul 17, Jul 28, Aug 1) and reducing early would have been wrong on every one. The rule is honoured. If it fires, the position comes off in full, without renegotiation.
